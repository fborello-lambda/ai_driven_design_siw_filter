## 0. Decision: dual-mode N=2 is the fabrication design

Compared all three current-best designs head to head — dual-mode N=2
(landed), cascade N=3 (best known), cascade N=4 (best known) — on identical
axes (`siw compare-designs`, figures at `docs/design_comparison_sparams.png`
and `docs/design_comparison_board_scale.png`):

| | Dual-mode N=2 | Cascade N=3 | Cascade N=4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| S11w | **−17.7dB** | −14.9dB | −6.1dB |
| IL | **−1.42dB** | −1.85dB | −2.7dB |
| BW vs 143.5MHz target | 318MHz (2.2×) | 189MHz (1.3×) | 200–216MHz (1.4–1.5×) |
| Board length | **119mm** | 172mm | 211mm |
| 0–8GHz spurious response | clean (next peak −3.7dB, well separated) | bad (second passband 5.75–6.44GHz, −1.78dB — nearly as strong as the main band) | bad (−4.05dB vs −2.80dB main band) |

**Dual-mode N=2 wins on every axis except bandwidth.** Both cascade designs
carry a second passband almost as strong as the intended one, which would
leak adjacent-band energy through in a real system; N=4's match is weak
enough to be borderline non-functional as a filter. Cascade work is now
**parked, not abandoned** — the numbers stand as a real negative/comparative
result for the paper (§5), but no further tuning effort goes into it unless
this decision is revisited.

Known open item carried forward on the chosen design: **bandwidth 318MHz vs
the 143.5MHz (5% FBW) target, 2.2× too wide.** Not fixed. A Qe(w_ext)
calibration for dual-mode (the same calibrate-and-invert treatment cascade
got) would be the principled way to close this, and does not exist yet —
`DualModeTopology.calibrate()` only does k12(w_iris).

## 1. Fabrication finish study — real findings, real blocker

### 1a. Soldermask/silkscreen — DONE, and a real bug was found and fixed

`topologies/dualmode.py`'s adapter was silently dropping the soldermask (and
via-conductivity) data before it reached the Palace config — the original
soldermask measurement was a no-op that happened to still produce numbers
(Palace defaults an untagged volume rather than erroring). Fixed
(`13af8cc`); real result, reproduced via the same wide-window method as the
bare-copper golden entry:

| | S11w | BW | IL |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bare copper (baseline) | −17.69dB | 318MHz | −1.42dB |
| +20µm soldermask (εr=3.5, tanδ=0.02) | **−13.59dB** | 303MHz | −1.46dB |

Bandwidth barely moves; return loss takes a real ~4dB hit from the added
capacitance under the feed trace. JLCPCB's actual soldermask (per
`jitxlib.jlcpcb.materials`) is closer to εr≈3.8 — our simulation is a touch
optimistic, so the real effect is probably marginally worse than −13.6dB.

### 1b. The real fab constraint: soldermask-off ≠ bare copper

Standard PCB process order: soldermask is applied **first** (openings only
at pads); a surface finish (ENIG/OSP/etc.) then plates **only the exposed
openings** — it does not go under the mask. So "remove the mask over the
feed" doesn't recover the ideal bare-copper number; it exposes that trace to
whatever finish the board uses. This reframes the fabrication question from
"mask vs. no mask" to "mask vs. finish", scoped in task #47:

| Cell | Status |
|---|---|
| Soldermask ON (bare Cu under it) | **done**, §1a |
| Soldermask OFF + OSP | feasible now — OSP is a thin dielectric (0.2–0.5µm), same machinery as §1a; its real εr/tanδ aren't pinned down yet |
| Soldermask OFF + ENIG | **blocked** — see §1c |
| Via fill (filled vs. unfilled) | **blocked** — see §1c, independent axis |

### 1c. Structural blocker found: ENIG and via-fill both need a geometry rework

Palace's `Boundaries.Conductivity` (surface-impedance BC) only applies to
true one-sided **exterior** boundaries (confirmed in
`~/palace/docs/src/reference.md` and by Palace's own MFEM warning: *"Found
boundary attribute with internal and external boundary elements"*). This
project's vias — and the copper trace itself — are modeled as internal,
two-sided PEC sheets cut through one continuous dielectric/air volume: cheap
and valid for ideal PEC (direction-agnostic), invalid for a lossy-conductor
BC. Task #46 tracks the fix (real excluded 3D conductor volumes, a genuine
one-sided exterior boundary) — it unlocks both the via-fill study (#43) and
the ENIG cell above, since they hit the identical wall. Not started; scope
not yet estimated, and it touches the core mesh-builder shared by every
simulation in the project, so it needs regression verification before
trusting anything built on it.

Until #46 lands, the *physically reasoned* (not simulated) expectation is
that ENIG is probably the safer choice for the launch region: its dominant
loss mechanism is ohmic (nickel's ~4× resistivity, industry data ~0.1–
0.2dB/inch above 1GHz), not the dielectric-detuning mechanism that costs the
soldermask its ~4dB S11w hit. Treat this as a hypothesis to confirm once #46
exists, not a substitute for the simulation.

## 5. Paper — "AI-Driven Design for SIW Filters"

Unchanged core material from `ROADMAP_2026-08-30.md` §6, plus two additions
from this session:

- **A second bug-taxonomy entry**: the dual-mode topology adapter silently
  dropped feature data between the mesh builder and the solver config for
  two separate studies (soldermask, via-conductivity) — a different failure
  mode than §0's five cascade bugs (those were bad *values*; this was a

  warned about it — a case for always checking the solver's own diagnostic
  output, not just whether it produced a number.
- The finish/soldermask study itself is a clean, real, honestly-quantified
  fabrication result either way it lands.

# ADDENDUM 2026-09-02 — real fabrication stack measured

## Results (dual-mode N=2, landed geometry, all `siw compare-fab-variants`)

| variant | fc (GHz) | IL (dB) | BW (MHz) | S11w (dB) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. PEC (as optimized) | 2.911 | −1.42 | 318 | −17.69 |
| 2. Cu+ENIG+mask+silk, vias **tented** | 2.909 | −1.79 | 298 | −12.48 |
| 3. Cu+ENIG+mask+silk, vias **open** | 2.909 | −1.79 | 298 | −12.47 |
| 4. Cu+ENIG+mask, **no silkscreen** | 2.907 | −1.79 | 297 | −12.50 |

Three findings, in order of importance:

1. **Return loss is the real fabrication penalty: −17.7 → −12.5 dB (+5.2 dB).**
   This is *detuning*, not loss — the ENIG/soldermask/silkscreen dielectric
   stack over the feed shifts the launch impedance. It puts the board
   **outside the −16 dB spec**, and it is the one effect worth acting on.
   Consistent with the earlier soldermask-only study (≈−4 dB); ENIG and
   silkscreen add roughly another 1 dB.
2. **Real copper costs 0.37 dB of insertion loss** (−1.42 → −1.79 dB), close
   to the 0.5–0.6 dB predicted from Qu 270 → ~188. Slightly under, as
   expected: only the exterior ground planes are lossy copper; the trace is
   still PEC. **Every golden saved before this date is PEC-only and
   understates IL by ~0.37 dB.**
3. **Via tenting and silkscreen are irrelevant.** All three real-stack
   variants agree to within 0.03 dB — below solver noise. Tented vs open
   vias differ by 0.01 dB, and that is an *upper bound* (see caveat), so the
   real difference is smaller still. **Do not pay for via filling/capping
   on RF grounds**, and silkscreen placement is a free choice.

## Modelling caveat (recorded in the golden entries)

The open-via aperture is modelled as an `Absorbing` boundary, which absorbs
all incident energy. The real 1.5 mm hole is ~40× below its own 117 GHz TE11
cutoff and radiates almost nothing, so this **overestimates** the open-via
penalty. That the overestimate is still only 0.01 dB is what makes the null
result trustworthy.

## Fabrication recommendation

- **Open (untented) vias, silkscreen as you like** — neither matters
  electrically.
- **Open the soldermask over the CPWG feed and SMA launch pad.** That is
  where the entire −5.2 dB return-loss penalty originates; the cavity region
  is unaffected because its fields are enclosed between ground planes.
- **ENIG for the finish** (board is unsoldered and exposed long-term; ENIG's
  gold/nickel is a genuine corrosion barrier, OSP is a short-term
  pre-solder film).

## Bugs found while getting here (7 this session)

All were silent — wrong physics, never an error. In this batch:
`silkscreen` domain had no material in the Palace config; CPWG
ground-stitch via walls were skipped from PEC with no conductor behind them;
via cap faces at z=0/z=h were swept into the interior barrel-wall branch.
Each left faces with **no boundary condition**, and Palace warned then
computed anyway — one produced IL *better than a lossless PEC structure*.
Two permanent safeguards now exist: a guard that raises if a mesh domain has
no material, and a catch-all that reports any unclassified exterior face.
