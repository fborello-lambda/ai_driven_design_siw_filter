The goal

A Substrate Integrated Waveguide (SIW) bandpass filter:

Parameter Target
Centre frequency 2.87 GHz
Bandwidth 143.5 MHz (5 % FBW)
Return loss at most −16 dB in band
Response Chebyshev, 0.1 dB ripple
Substrate Rogers RO4350B, 0.76 mm, 0.5 oz copper (17.5 µm)
Vias Ø1.5 mm, 3.0 mm pitch
Connector Taoglas EMPCB.SMAFSTJ.B.HT, CPWG launch

For what to do next, see PLAN.md. Superseded documents are in docs/archive/.

The design

Dual-mode N=2 — a single square SIW cavity carrying two degenerate modes, split by an internal floating iris row.

Rounding every dimension to 0.1 mm PCB resolution costs only 0.5 dB (−10.25 dB, 319 MHz), so the design is not perched on a knife edge.

The central finding: bandwidth and match are in opposition

The specification asks for a 143.5 MHz band and −16 dB return loss. On this topology those two are in direct conflict, and that is a measured result, not a tuning failure.

Four one-dimensional scans on the assembled filter (not a proxy structure), every point fine-mesh and verified:

w_ext at w_iris = 11 mm: 120.7 / 127.7 / 145.6 / 166.4 / 366.5 MHz for 13 / 14.5 / 15.5 / 16 / 17.5 mm, return loss never better than −2.20 dB.

y_wall at w_iris = 11 mm: 147.1 / 146.7 / 153.6 / 169.1 MHz for 7.5 / 9.5 / 11 / 13 mm, return loss −1.77 to −2.27 dB — nearly inert.

Bandwidth alone is solvable. At w_iris = 11 mm the filter measures 145.6 MHz against a 143.5 MHz target — FBW 4.94 % against 5 %. The band was never “stuck”; it spans 35–366 MHz across the geometries scanned. What cannot be done is reaching that bandwidth and a usable match: return loss improves monotonically in whichever direction widens the band, on every parameter, with no interior optimum.

The project has chosen the good-match end (2026-09-03). The best return loss this structure produces anywhere is −10.76 dB.

A 30-evaluation local Bayesian search over a 5-dimensional ±2 mm box confirmed this geometry rather than improving it: neighbouring probes came back worse, the search spent its last evaluations resampling one point, and the 0.41 dB apparent gain over the previous best sits inside the measured coarse-mesh noise floor (re-sampling one geometry within 0.2 mm moved it 0.64 dB).

What the fabrication stack costs

Real copper: −0.32 dB insertion loss, −0.21 dB return loss. Centre frequency, bandwidth and pole structure all unchanged. This is the one trustworthy fabrication number, measured at the final geometry with the exterior ground planes modelled as lossy copper via a surface-impedance boundary condition.

Soldermask, silkscreen and ENIG cannot be modelled at this mesh density and are excluded. This was established by a one-factor-at-a-time ladder, each cell adding exactly one ingredient to the idealised baseline:

ingredient thickness ΔIL vs PEC return loss reflection zeros
copper (boundary condition, no geometry) — −0.32 −10.55 2 ✓
soldermask 20 µm −2.54 −0.98 3
silkscreen 12 µm −4.02 −0.54 0
ENIG 4.5 µm −7.19 −0.18 0

The damage scales monotonically with thinness, across two dielectrics and a conductor whose material properties have nothing in common. No physical mechanism does that; unresolved sliver elements do. Each layer spans the whole board inside a mesh whose finest cell is 200 µm, and adding one raises the degree-of-freedom count by only about 3 % — they are represented by a handful of cells with aspect ratios of 50:1 to 270:1. Resolving a 4.5 µm layer honestly would need roughly 1 µm cells, a thousand-fold local refinement.

The numbers in that table are therefore not fabrication penalties. They are the signature of a model outside its validity, and they are kept in the figure precisely so nobody re-adds those layers expecting meaning.

Consequences for the fabrication decisions

Choice Decision Basis
Finish ENIG board is unsoldered and exposed; gold/nickel is a real corrosion barrier. RF effect negligible — the plating sits on the air side of the ground planes, so cavity fields see copper first
Soldermask over the feed open it engineering judgement, not simulation: a 20 µm εr 3.8 film across the 0.2512 mm coplanar gap perturbs a launch that is already the weakest part of the design
Silkscreen free choice not measurable here; physically negligible
Vias tented vs open unanswerable the hollow-barrel geometry only exists together with a volumetric conductor model that is itself unresolvable

The earlier pad-only-launch fabrication study is superseded — its cells ran through the same unresolved-layer path. Its one figure that survives scrutiny is “+0.37 dB from real copper”, close to today’s properly measured 0.32 dB.

What is true right now

Solid

  • The geometry above is a local optimum for return loss under the CPWG launch, confirmed by a 30-evaluation search rather than assumed.
  • It is robust to PCB tolerances: 0.1 mm rounding costs 0.5 dB.
  • Real copper costs 0.32 dB and does not disturb the response shape.
  • The drawn geometry matches the meshed geometry — siw stackup --verify checks ground-stitch vias (16), fence vias (78), the 10-vertex feed outline including the CPWG pad taper, and the coplanar gap, all passing.

Not true, and now understood

  • Return loss misses spec: −10.76 dB against −16 dB.
  • Bandwidth misses spec: 316.6 MHz against 143.5 MHz.
  • These are one trade, not two faults. Either can be met alone; neither can be met together on this topology. Meeting the bandwidth costs the match entirely (−1.75 dB at 145.6 MHz).
  • The fabrication stack is unquantified beyond copper, and cannot be quantified without a mesh this machine cannot build.
  • No board has been ordered. No CST export, no gerbers, no DRC check.

The one habit that matters

Fifteen silent bugs have now been found in this project’s own model. Not one raised an error; every one produced plausible numbers that were wrong. Today added four, and the last of them took three attempts to diagnose:

  • an objective term that silently steered every search away from the best design, because optimize() never forwarded the weights it accepted;
  • a “bandwidth curve” that was really the width of one of two disjoint passbands at three of its four points, so inverting it aimed the search at a discontinuity;
  • an exterior-face flag hardcoded True, correct for a bare board and wrong the moment layers sit above it;
  • thin fabrication layers meshing as slivers and destroying the response.

Three lessons, each paid for today:

  1. If a fix does not change the number, the diagnosis was wrong. Two confident explanations of the −29 dB stack failure were falsified this way, each in one re-run.
  2. After two wrong inferences, stop inferring. The one-factor ladder settled in four solves what argument had failed to settle twice — and it immediately exonerated the leading suspect.
  3. A correct mechanism aimed at the wrong component is still wrong. The skin-depth arithmetic was right; it was applied to the vias, which were fine, instead of to the layers, which were not.

Before trusting any result, check it against what physics says it should be. Insertion loss cannot beat a lossless model. A lossy stack cannot cost 27 dB. Pole count must match the filter order. A hole far below cutoff cannot radiate.

Guards now in the code:

  • _driven_config raises if a mesh domain has no material;
  • the mesh builder reports any unclassified exterior face, and raises on an empty mesh instead of handing Palace something valid-looking but empty;
  • band_edges reports n_lobes, so a split response can never again be read as a narrow one;
  • siw scan excludes split points from its inversion and says why;
  • the fabrication runner flags any variant whose loss beats the lossless reference, or is more than 6 dB worse, or shows near-total reflection, or reports zero reflection zeros;
  • siw stackup --verify compares the drawing against the meshed geometry vertex by vertex.

Reproducing this

# the design
siw design --topology dualmode --N 2 \
    --seed a=57.21,w_iris=15.26,x_iris=28.68,y_wall=8.62,w_ext=16.31 \
    --span 2 --patience 14 --evals 30 --w-bw 0 --device CPU --np 8

# the fabrication study
siw compare-fab-variants --run \
    --params a=57.158,w_iris=15.304,x_iris=28.685,y_wall=9.849,w_ext=16.352 \
    --N 2 --device CPU --np 4

# the trade-off scans
siw scan --topology dualmode --N 2 \
    --params a=57.21,x_iris=28.68,y_wall=8.62,w_ext=15.5 \
    --scan 'w_iris=11,12,13,14' --device CPU --np 8

# artifacts
siw layout --topology dualmode --N 2 \
    --params a=57.158,w_iris=15.304,x_iris=28.685,y_wall=9.849,w_ext=16.352
siw stackup --verify

Resource limits, learned the hard way: fabrication-stack solves need --np 4 on this 15 GB machine — eight ranks are killed mid-sweep by the OOM killer — and cost about 32 minutes each.
